How many calories do exercise games burn?

Movus guides · Updated 2026-11-05

Exercise games can be a genuine workout — but the number on screen is only as good as the method behind it. Here is how Movus estimates it, and what you can expect from each game.

How the estimate works

Every activity has a MET value: how many times your resting metabolism it takes. Running is high, sitting is 1. Movus gives each game a MET based on the Compendium of Physical Activities and on what the game actually asks of your body.

It then estimates your resting metabolism from your height, weight, age and sex using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, and counts only the active part — (MET − 1) × resting rate × minutes. That is the same thing Apple Health calls active energy, so the two agree.

Typical figures

For a 35-year-old, 170 cm, 70 kg adult, ten minutes of continuous play:

Slash4.5≈37

A heavier or more muscular person burns more for the same game; a lighter person burns less. Movus uses your own numbers from Apple Health if you allow it.

What makes the real number higher or lower

  • How much you move. A round you barely moved in burns less, and Movus logs the movements it actually read.
  • Rest between rounds. The table assumes continuous play.
  • Depth and range. Deeper squats and bigger arm strokes cost more.

Is it enough to count as exercise?

Most adults are advised to get around 150 minutes of moderate activity a week. Activities from 3 to 6 MET count as moderate and anything above 6 as vigorous, so every Movus game is squarely moderate activity and the hardest two reach the vigorous line. Fifteen to twenty minutes a day counts toward that 150.

We would rather under-promise here. These figures are deliberately conservative: an active game is not the sport it imitates — there is no ground to cover and nothing to push against — and measured studies of camera-controlled games put them well below the equivalent real activity. Your own watch, if you wear one, is a better number than ours.
